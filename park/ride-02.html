Ride · 02

The Perceiver
Problem

The observer is never outside the observation. Their knowledge, their exposure to media and academic frameworks, their prior experiences — all of it shapes what they see before they see it. This is not a flaw in the observer. It is a variable that must be accounted for in every elimination process.

The Challenge
"Usually mixed by knowledge of perceiver — if you don't know everything, you think otherworldly. Especially with influence from media or academia." — John Ernest Carter · Field Notes

Part One — The Media Filter

A person who has watched 200 hours of UFO documentary content before their first field observation is not starting from zero. They have been pre-loaded with templates — shapes, behaviors, and narratives — that their pattern recognition will attempt to match against anything anomalous they encounter.

Describe precisely how this pre-loading degrades the reliability of their observations. Then describe what would need to be true for their observations to be considered useful data anyway.

Part Two — The Academic Filter

Institutional science has a consensus position on anomalous phenomena. A researcher trained inside that consensus carries it into every observation — often unconsciously. They will reach for prosaic explanations not because the evidence demands it but because their training makes prosaic explanations feel more legitimate regardless of evidence quality.

Explain how this filter produces false Category I results. Give a specific example of an observation that an academically trained researcher would incorrectly eliminate as prosaic that a field-trained researcher with a proper elimination catalog would not.

Part Three — The Subtraction of Self

The subtraction methodology was not only developed for anomalous phenomena research. It was lived — applied to the researcher's own identity and experience across decades of not fitting available explanations for who he was and what he observed.

Explain why a methodology learned through personal experience of systematic rejection produces more reliable observational frameworks than one derived from literature alone. What does rejection teach that acceptance cannot?

What a passing answer looks like: Specific, mechanistic explanations of how each filter operates. Not "bias affects observation" — that is noise. How. Exactly. Step by step. The personal subtraction question requires genuine engagement, not academic rephrasing.
