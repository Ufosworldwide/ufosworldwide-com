Ride · 03

The Four
Categories

The elimination framework has four categories. They are not suggestions. They are a complete taxonomy. Every anomalous observation belongs to one of them. The work is determining which one — and showing every elimination step that leads to that determination.

The Framework
I
Prosaic
Explained by known natural or man-made phenomena. Bugs, bats, birds, lens artifacts, atmospheric distortion, compression errors, aircraft, satellites, weather balloons. Most observations resolve here.
II
Concealed or Chronic
Known but not publicly acknowledged. Classified programs, experimental aircraft, directed energy systems, surveillance platforms. Real but not what the observer thinks it is.
III
Industry Artifact
Equipment, military, or infrastructure-related phenomena misidentified as anomalous. Drone swarms, laser systems, industrial equipment, telecommunications infrastructure.
IV
Genuine Anomaly
Survives full subtraction against all known categories. Rare by design. The rarity is what gives it meaning. A methodology that produces frequent Category IV results has insufficient elimination criteria.

The Assignment

Apply the complete elimination framework to each of the three observations below. Work through every category in sequence. Do not skip to your conclusion. The path is the answer.

For each observation: attempt Category I elimination first. If it survives, attempt Category II. Then III. What remains after all three elimination attempts is your category assignment — with the evidence for why each category failed to eliminate it.
